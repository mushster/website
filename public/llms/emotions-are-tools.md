# Emotions are tools

Humans are spectacular because we wield tools. A hammer, a lever, a spreadsheet, a language. We pick each one up for a job and set it down when the job is done. Nobody confuses themselves with the hammer.

I think emotions belong on the same shelf.

## Picking one up

Anger is useful. It tells you a line has been crossed and it gives you the energy to say so. Longing is useful. It points at what you actually want, which is often different from what you have been telling people you want. Sonder, the sudden sense that every stranger has a life as full as yours, is useful when you have stopped being kind.

Each of these does a specific job. Anger is bad at planning. Longing is bad at gratitude. Sonder is bad at deadlines. You would not use a saw to drive a nail, and you should not use anger to write the email.

## Putting it down

The hard part is the second half. A tool you cannot put down is no longer a tool. It is a weight, and after long enough it is a personality.

Most of the damage I have seen people do, myself included, came from holding an emotion past the point where it was doing any work. The anger that was right on Tuesday was still in hand on Friday, aimed at someone who had nothing to do with it.

Putting it down does not mean pretending you never held it. It means noticing that the job is finished, or that this was the wrong tool for it, and opening your hand.

## What this is not

This is not an argument for feeling less. A carpenter with more tools is a better carpenter. The aim is range and a loose grip: to be able to reach for grief, or joy, or fury when the moment calls for it, and to still be the one holding it.

Pick it up when you need it. Put it down before it picks you up.
